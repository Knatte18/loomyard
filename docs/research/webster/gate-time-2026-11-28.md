# Where implementer rows spend their gate time, 2026-11-28

What a Webster or Weaver row costs since the card gate also runs the packages that depend on a card's packages
(`a69580709`, landed 2026-11-27 07:14 UTC), and which cuts the records support.
It reads records only: no agent, bench or gate ran for it.
Every figure is a measurement of past runs at one point in time.

## Findings

- A row after the change spends 7.1 min per card in gates, fixers and slot waits, against 4.8 min before it.
- The card gate's listed tier run went from 5.5 s to 44.8 s per run.
  A card's own packages are 2.3 of the 75.5 packages that run now.
- The batch gate went from 6.6 to 24.1 min per row, mostly its tier1 run over the dependent packages.
  None of its tier1 failures after the change was in a dependent package.
- The dependent packages do catch failures at the card gate:
  10 of the 23 failed listed runs outside fixer sessions failed only there.
- Cold fixers take 21.9 min per row, of which 12.6 min is card gate runs they make themselves before the gate runs again.
- The largest cuts are the batch gate's tier1 scope (about 15 min per row), the card gate's scope (9 to 14 min)
  and the fixers' own card gate runs (about 11 min).
  They overlap and do not add up.

## Sample and method

- **Rows.** Every Webster and Weaver row of this hub that ended between 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-28 12:34 UTC.
  Five ended before `a69580709` landed and six after it.
  One Webster row was still running at the end of the window and is left out.
  Four of the six later rows are the bench runs of [bench-2026-11-27](../weaver/bench-2026-11-27/report.md),
  which implemented older plans again.
- **Row time.** The time the run's history shows the implementer row running, plan-level verify included.
  Time the row stood blocked is left out, and so is machine hibernation, read from the system journal
  (516 min of `dobby-run-watch-build`).
- **Gate runs.** The hub's gate run records: gate key, tier, command, wait start, start, wall time, outcome,
  failed tests and per-package test times.
- **Cold fixers.** Session transcripts whose prompt opens as a cold gate fixer, first to last timestamp.
  The one verify-fix fork of the sample (`config-keys-nested`) is counted with them.
- **Split.** Each instant of a row counts once, in this order: fixer session, plan-level verify, batch gate, card gate,
  other test runs, gate-slot wait, agent work.
  A fixer's own gate runs are therefore fixer time.
  Agent work is the rest, so it also holds the lint steps, the scope computation and every error of the other columns.
- **Card packages.** The packages of the targets the plan names for the cards of a gate call.
  Calls are matched to run records by their time in the transcripts.

a tier0 run of the `//lym:guard` tests the listed run leaves out,

## Cold fixers inside the rows

- The card gate hands a failed call to a cold fixer.
  Its prompt (`contracts/stencils/gatefix/gatefix-body-card.md`) has it re-run the failed check
  and then the whole card gate call.
- In 62 fixer sessions after the change that ran a card gate, the fixers' own card gate runs took 75.7 min
  (12.6 min per row), against 35.0 min (7.0 per row) before.
- Each of those sessions was followed within a minute by another card gate call that ran in full,
  56.9 min over the six later rows.
  That second call is the one that judges the fix.

## New levers

- **A. Batch gate tier1 over the batch's own packages.**
  The tier1 listed run takes 18.9 min per row.
  At the 32.6 s per run it took over own packages, it would take 4.3 min: **14.6 min saved**.
  No tier1 failure of the sample was in a dependent, and the plan-level verify still runs tier1 over everything.
- **B. The card fixer stops at the failed check.**
  It would leave the whole card gate to the call that judges the fix: **about 11 min saved**.
  The ceiling is the 12.6 min of own card gate runs; what the narrower re-check costs instead is assumed, not measured.
  A fix that breaks something else then costs another fixer or fork round, which is not estimated.
- **C. Card gate over the card's own packages, dependents at the batch gate.**
  The listed run would fall from 20.6 to about 2.5 min per row.
  The guard step would take back about 4.4 min, since fewer guard tests are covered by the listed run.
  The 10 dependent-only failures (1.7 per row) would surface at the batch gate, at up to 3.0 min each:
  **9 to 14 min saved**.
- **D. One compile step in tier1.**
  [gate-fixed-cost-2026-11-27](../speed/gate-fixed-cost-2026-11-27/report.md) measured that replacing
  `go build ./... && go vet {targets}` with `go vet ./...` saves 2 to 3 s per call,
  at the price of not linking commands that have no tests.
  A row makes 35 to 47 tier1 runs, without and with its fixer sessions' runs: **about 1.7 min saved**.

## Limits

- Six rows after the change and five before, with different plans; one row (`push-retry-not-halt`) is a fifth of all time.
- Every saving is an estimate from per-run times of other runs or another side; none was run.
- Slot wait is measured from a run record's wait start to its start, so it holds any preparation made between them.
- `async-run-pusher` changed Master mid-row; its 30 min between the two is counted as agent work.
- Fixer sessions are matched to rows by time and worktree, and their length includes model turns and gate runs alike.
